Built for the café, not the corporation.

A café owner or a two-person trades company usually hears about a grant after the deadline, or never. Consultants exist, but few small businesses can afford one for a $5,000 application. GrantMatch does the searching, the eligibility rules and the first draft. The owner answers questions, reviews, and submits.

It gets smarter with every application.

Grant listings are mostly public. Which applications actually got funded isn't. Every application through GrantMatch teaches us what wins, so our matches and drafts improve with every customer. A newcomer starts with none of that.

Next: how it works

Profile to funded.
Four steps.

GrantMatch is a web app that works on any phone or laptop. Here's what a business owner does, and what happens behind the scenes.

1

Build your profile.

Ten minutes of plain questions: industry, province, number of employees, revenue range, and what you plan to do this year, such as hiring, exporting or going digital.

2

Get your matches.

We check your profile against every program in our database and rank each one by fit, amount and deadline, with a plain-language reason for every match.

3

Review the draft.

Pick a grant and GrantMatch drafts the application from your answers. On paid plans, a human grant advisor reviews it before you see it.

4

Submit and track.

You submit through the funder's own portal. We remind you of deadlines, follow-ups and the reports most grants require after the money arrives.

What you get.

  • Money you didn't know you qualified for.
  • A weekend of reading program guides turned into an hour of review.
  • No missed deadlines or follow-up reports.
  • Nothing to pay up front on the pay-when-funded plan.

Where we operate.

GrantMatch is built and run from a small office in Vancouver, BC, and offered online across Canada. There's no physical product and nothing to warehouse. Our inventory is the grant database, stored on Canadian cloud servers.

We launch in British Columbia first, where we know the programs best, then add provinces one at a time.

One platform. Both sides win.

Work-to-earn scholarships connect our two audiences. Here's how a café on GrantMatch ends up helping pay a student's tuition.

1 · Business

A café owner finds a youth hiring subsidy.

GrantMatch matches the café to a wage subsidy for hiring a young worker, and drafts the application.

2 · Offer

GrantMatch suggests a work-to-earn scholarship.

The café posts a part-time job that comes with $1,500 toward tuition after 300 hours. Hiring and training new staff is expensive, and a scholarship gives students a reason to stay.

3 · Student

Students who qualify see it first.

It appears in GrantMatch Students for nearby students whose schedule and program fit. They apply with the profile they already have.

4 · Result

Everyone gets something.

The student gets a paid job and tuition money. The café gets a worker who stays, with part of the wage covered where the subsidy's rules allow. GrantMatch earns the $500 hire fee plus the success fee on the subsidy.

Who pays.

  • Employers who need part-time staff, like cafés, retail and trades, using scholarships to hire and keep students.
  • Companies with scholarship programs that want more qualified applicants.
  • Foundations and community groups whose awards go unclaimed because nobody hears about them.
  • Colleges promoting their own bursaries and entrance awards.

Sponsored never means better.

  • Sponsored opportunities are always labelled.
  • They're only shown to people who meet the rules.
  • They never push a better-fitting match lower on the list.
  • We never sell student data. A partner sees a student only when that student chooses to apply.
  • Students never pay.

Success fees are invoiced to the business after the money arrives. Offering a scholarship or grant instead? See partner pricing.

Why this price.

Small businesses are short on cash, so the main plan costs nothing until it works. That also tells customers we only win when they do.

On a typical $15,000 grant, our fee is $1,200, far less than paying a consultant by the hour. The cap keeps large awards fair.

We follow funder rules.

Some programs don't allow grant money to pay for application help, and grant-writing ethics codes discourage percentage fees. So our fee is always billed to the business, never taken out of the grant. If a program forbids success fees, that application moves to a flat fee or the Pro plan.

Our process, every month.

We decided by consensus, not by vote. AI acted like an outside expert we could question, but the decisions were ours.

1

Analyze the report.

We read the management report together: sales, returns, costs, and every store's prices and profit.

2

Talk it through.

We discussed what happened and listed options for prices, orders, staffing and advertising.

3

Check with AI.

We asked an AI assistant to check our options for missing information, using the manual's rules and our past results. Our instructor allowed this.

4

Decide together.

We made the final call as a group and filled in the team decision form.

Conflicts, and how we resolved them.

We had no major conflicts. When someone disagreed or was unsure, we checked the question against the data before deciding.

  • Pastry prices. Why charge so much for pastries? We compared the profit per dozen at different prices before agreeing.
  • Our best seller. Wasn't cake the most popular? The sales history showed raised donuts sold the most.
  • Returns. Could we really return leftovers for 80% credit? We found the rule in the manual before ordering extra stock.
  • Competitor prices. What if rivals kept prices low and took our customers? Market share across stores barely moved with price.

Meetings.

We met mostly in class, where we reviewed each report together, and kept working in an Instagram group chat between classes.

Questions got answered quickly, nobody waited a week for a decision, and everyone took part in agreeing on each form.
