The problem in numbers

Most owners never hear about new programs.

When Ottawa launched the Regional Tariff Response Initiative to help businesses hurt by tariffs, over three quarters of small business owners didn't know it existed, and fewer than 1% had applied. In another survey, not knowing about the programs was the top reason (68%) small businesses didn't use government training support. And only 7% of Canadian small and medium businesses asked for any government financing in 2023.

Sources: CFIB, A Divided Year (2026) and Canada's Training Ground (2025); Statistics Canada, Survey on Financing and Growth of SMEs, 2023. All sources

Built for the café, not the corporation.

A café owner or a two-person trades company usually hears about a grant after the deadline, or never. Consultants exist, but few small businesses can afford one for a $5,000 application. GrantRadar does the searching, the eligibility rules and the first draft. The owner answers questions, reviews, and submits.

It gets smarter with every application.

We start with public data. The federal government publishes who received its grants over $25,000 (the Grants and Contributions dataset on open.canada.ca), so we can see which kinds of businesses win the larger programs. Smaller awards and rejections aren't published, so every application we help with adds our own funded or rejected results, which nobody else has.

How it works

Profile to funded.
Four steps.

GrantRadar is a web app that works on any phone or laptop. Here's what a business owner does, and what happens behind the scenes.

1

Build your profile.

Ten minutes of plain questions: industry, location, number of employees, revenue range, and what you plan to do this year, such as hiring, exporting or going digital.

2

Get your matches.

We check your profile against every program in our database and rank each one by fit, amount and deadline, with a plain-language reason for every match.

3

Pick a grant and get your draft.

Choose a grant and see its flat fee before you start. Pay once, answer a few questions, and GrantRadar drafts the application from your answers. A human grant advisor reviews it before you see it.

4

Submit and track.

You submit through the funder's own portal. We remind you of deadlines, follow-ups and the reports most grants require after the money arrives.

What you get.

  • Money you didn't know you qualified for.
  • A weekend of reading program guides turned into an hour of review.
  • No missed deadlines or follow-up reports.
  • One flat fee, known before you start. You keep every dollar you win.

Where we operate.

GrantRadar is built and run from a small office in Vancouver, BC, and offered online. There's no physical product and nothing to warehouse. Our inventory is the grant database, stored on Canadian cloud servers.

We sell direct to customers online. No wholesaler or retailer. Accountants and business groups refer customers to us, but the business always buys from us directly.

We launch in British Columbia, where we know the programs best, then add Alberta and Ontario in Year 2.

Our rule

You submit. We never lobby.

We never contact government officials on a client's behalf. The business submits its own application. That keeps GrantRadar clear of lobbying rules. Our fee is for writing help, never a commission on the grant.

Why $3 million.

$600,000 for 20% values GrantRadar at $3 million after the investment. That's about 1.5 times our Year 2 revenue target of $2.0 million, a modest multiple for a software company growing this fast. In Year 3, your 20% share of profit would be about $260,000 a year, with the company still growing.

What could go wrong, and our plan.

  • Fewer applications than planned. If Year 2 has 1,800 applications instead of 2,400 (25% fewer), we'd lose about $85,000 if we changed nothing. Delaying two of the six Year 2 hires saves about $120,000, bringing us to about break-even with over $300,000 still in the bank.
  • Clients apply once and leave. Loyalty rewards, a saved profile and new-program alerts bring them back for their next grant, and our targets don't count on extra repeat business.
  • A program changes its rules. Researchers check every program monthly.
  • A big competitor copies us. Our funded-or-rejected data keeps improving, and they start from zero.

Per application.

Each application brings in $734 on average after discounts. Direct costs are about $100 (advisor time, AI and card fees), so about 86% of every fee goes toward salaries and growth.

With Year 2's full team and costs, we break even at about 1,920 applications a year, which is 80% of our Year 2 target.

Why loyalty rewards cost us little.

  • Saved profiles, early alerts, check-ups and a dedicated advisor cost almost nothing. The software does the work, or it's an advisor we already pay.
  • Priority review only changes the order of the queue.
  • Second Shot reuses a draft we already wrote, so it's half the work for half the price.
  • Referral credits replace ad spend. A $150 referral costs less than finding a customer through ads.
  • Discounts only start after a client has already paid us 3 or 6 times, so they reward revenue we already have.

How we made
decisions.

Before GrantRadar, we ran a donut shop called Donuts Delight in the Enterprise Donuts business simulation. The simulation's final report named us the top profit producer.

Final profit, June to October$8,040

"The top profit producer was store #4," said the final report. That's us.

Vs. the computer's Company Store137%

We made $1.37 for every $1.00 the computer-run store made ($8,040 to $5,855).

Missed sales0

In all five months, and the trial. No customer ever left without being served.

How the simulation worked.

Each month, we made the same set of decisions. The computer then played out the month and sent back a report showing what we sold, what was left over, and how much we made.

What we decided every month.

  • Prices for four products: cake donuts, raised donuts, fancy donuts and pastries.
  • How many dozen to buy from the bakery.
  • Staff hours, and what share of staff were full-time.
  • Advertising, and whether to buy market research reports on competitors.

The four rules that shaped our choices.

  • Bulk discount. Buying 2,000 dozen or more in a month took 5% off everything we bought.
  • Returns. Unsold donuts could be returned for 80% of what we paid, so leftovers only cost us 20%.
  • Staff limit. Our staff hours set a "stretch point": the most dozens our staff could serve. Past it, customers walk out unserved. Those are "missed sales."
  • Unhappy customers. Missed sales make customers buy less the next month, so running out hurts twice.

Our process, every month.

We decided by consensus, meaning everyone had to agree, not by majority vote. AI acted like an outside expert we could question, but the decisions were ours.

1

Analyze the report.

We read the month's report together: sales, leftovers, costs, and, when we bought market research, every store's prices and share of customers.

2

Talk it through.

We discussed what happened and why, then listed options for prices, orders, staff hours and advertising.

3

Check with AI.

We asked an AI assistant to check our options against the simulation manual's rules and our past results, to catch anything we missed. The course guidelines encourage using AI.

4

Decide together.

We made the final call as a group. Once everyone agreed, our team leader, Nick, filled in the team decision form.

Disagreements, and how we settled them.

We had no major conflicts. When someone disagreed or wasn't sure, we settled it with the numbers in our reports or the rules in the manual, not by arguing.

  • "Are our prices too high?" In June we charged more than most rivals, yet every store still got about 20% of customers (19.2% to 21.5%). That showed customers barely switched stores over price, so higher prices meant more profit, not fewer customers.
  • "Isn't cake the most popular?" Our reports showed raised donuts outsold every other product, every month (for example, 612 dozen in July against 365 for cake). So we always ordered the most raised donuts.
  • "Why buy more than we can sell?" We bought 2,010 dozen most months while selling about 1,600 to 1,800. We checked the manual: leftovers return for 80% credit. The 5% bulk discount on every dozen saved more than the 20% we lost on returns, and we never ran out.
  • "What if rivals undercut us?" Share of customers barely moved when other stores changed prices, so we didn't chase their low prices.

Meetings and participation.

We met mostly in class, where we reviewed each report together, and kept working in an Instagram group chat between classes.

Everyone joined in. We threw ideas around in our discussions and went through the pros and cons of each option, so no one was left on the sidelines. Questions got answered quickly, and nobody waited a week for a decision.

At launch, GrantRadar is only for small businesses. Once the business side is working, the same matching can serve two more groups. Neither is counted in our revenue targets.

GrantRadar Students

Free scholarship matching.

Students get matched to scholarships, bursaries and student grants, with a deadline calendar and feedback on their essays. We don't write scholarship essays, since most awards require the student's own work. Students never pay.

GrantRadar Partners

Companies pay to reach who qualifies.

Employers and foundations pay to list scholarships: $300 a month for a featured listing, or $20 per qualified applicant. Employers can also offer "work-to-earn" scholarships, such as $1,500 toward tuition after 300 hours, for a $500 fee per student hired. Sponsored listings are always labelled and only shown to people who qualify.

Privacy policy · sample for a concept business

Privacy policy

GrantRadar collects only the business information needed to match you to grants and draft applications: your business profile, your answers to our questions, and the documents you choose to upload.

How we use it

To find programs you qualify for, draft your applications, remind you of deadlines, and improve our matching using results from past applications, with names and identifying details removed.

Who we share it with

No one, unless you ask us to. You submit your own applications to funders. We never sell your data. Our suppliers (cloud hosting and an AI model provider) process data only to run GrantRadar, and data is stored on Canadian servers.

Your rights

We follow BC's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) and Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA). You can see, correct, download or delete your data at any time from your account settings.

Terms · sample for a concept business

Terms of service

What we do

GrantRadar matches businesses to grant programs and drafts applications from the information you provide. You review, approve and submit every application yourself. We never contact government officials on your behalf, and we can't guarantee that any application will be funded.

Fees

Application fees are $399, $899 or $1,799, set by the program's maximum award and shown before you buy. They're charged at checkout, before your finished draft is released, and aren't refundable once the draft is released. Fees don't depend on whether you're funded. GrantRadar never takes a share of your grant. Fees are billed to your business and shouldn't be claimed as a grant expense unless the program allows it. Pro is $149 a month with a 6-month minimum.

Loyalty rewards

Loyalty tiers are based on applications bought in the last 12 months. Discounts don't stack. You get the largest one you qualify for. Referral credits have no cash value. We may change rewards with 30 days' notice. Discounts already earned stay valid.

Your responsibilities

The information in your applications must be true and complete. You're responsible for checking each draft before you submit it. If an application form asks whether anyone helped prepare it, answer honestly.

Referrals

If an accountant or business group referred you, we may pay them a referral fee. We'll tell you when that applies.
